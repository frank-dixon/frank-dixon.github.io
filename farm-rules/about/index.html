Livestock Rules lists federal, Massachusetts, and local rules that bear on raising, processing, and selling animals. It is free. This copy runs in your browser. Accounts come later. Lookups you save are kept on this device. There is no paid tier.

A result is a set of summaries. Each summary has a citation, a source link, the date it was last checked, and an office to confirm it with. The wording does not say that a person or an operation is in the clear.

What is on file

Massachusetts statutes, Code of Massachusetts Regulations, and Department of Agricultural Resources and Department of Public Health guidance that bear on keeping animals, zoning, slaughter and processing, selling, eggs, and licensing. Federal rules are included where they sit next to those state rules: the poultry exemptions in 9 CFR 381.10 and the cattle exemptions in 9 CFR 303.1.

Town and city bylaws, ordinances, and board of health rules are on file for these municipalities only:

  • Amherst, Hampshire County
  • Chicopee, Hampden County
  • Hadley, Hampshire County
  • Leverett, Franklin County
  • South Hadley, Hampshire County
  • Southwick, Hampden County

The town table is not limited to that list. Another municipality is a new row, plus sources and rules, not a change to the model.

Every lookup shows the federal layer, the Massachusetts layer, the county layer, and the town layer. An empty layer means nothing is on file. It is not a finding that no rule exists.

Sources

The catalog points at official and community documents:

  • Massachusetts Legislature, for the General Laws, including chapters 40A, 94, and 101.
  • The Code of Massachusetts Regulations, read through the Cornell Legal Information Institute and the Mass.gov regulation pages, including 105 CMR 500 and 330 CMR 5.
  • Mass.gov publications from the Department of Agricultural Resources and the Department of Public Health, including egg guidance, farmers-market guidance, and policy FP-03.
  • The Code of Federal Regulations, read through the Cornell Legal Information Institute, for 9 CFR 381.10 and 9 CFR 303.1.
  • Town and city sites, and eCode360, for adopted bylaws and ordinances.
  • The Massachusetts Association of Agricultural Commissions, for a community copy of the Hadley right-to-farm bylaw. That entry is marked unverified.
  • UMass Extension. The crops, dairy, livestock, and equine page is collected so a later check can see whether it changed. Text from that page is not turned into a rule on its own.

A draft that has not been adopted is not loaded as a rule. A phone number or email is included only when it was on the page that was checked.

How an entry is checked

Checked means a person read the cited source and wrote the summary in the catalog, on the date shown. Unverified means the summary is on file but was not confirmed against the adopted text. A caveat on the card says so. Do not rely on an unverified card.

How updates work

python manage.py scrape_sources fetches each catalog URL, stores the raw text, and stores a SHA-256 hash. If a later run sees a different hash, the source is marked changed. The previous hash is kept. A failed fetch stores the error and leaves the last good text in place.

python manage.py parse_sources marks a rule for review when its document changed, or when a phrase the summary depends on is no longer in the text. It does not write a new summary. A changed source stays a changed source until a person reads it, edits the catalog, and runs python manage.py seed_rules.

Schedule scrape_sources and then parse_sources. Do not schedule seed_rules. That command loads the checked catalog and clears the review flag, which is a human step.

Nothing in this process is a claim that the file is complete, current, or sufficient for a decision.
