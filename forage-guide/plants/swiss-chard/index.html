Beta vulgaris · beet greens

A garden green that is useful and high in oxalate.

Who can eat it

  • Rabbit Limit

    Chard and beet greens are high in oxalate. House Rabbit Society lists them as an occasional green, not a staple.

    Do not feed them every day, and do not combine them with a pile of dock or purslane.

Identification

Large shiny leaves with a thick white, yellow, or red midrib, on a plant you planted. Beet leaves count.

Lookalikes

Rhubarb also has a thick stalk, and the leaf is toxic. Rhubarb leaves are broader and the plant is a large clump that dies to the ground. Do not mix them up.

Parts to feed

Leaves, sparingly.

New England timing

Leaves are cut from July until frost. Beet greens are the same plant species and the same oxalate limit.

Harvest

Cut a few leaves, not a daily diet of chard.

Preparation

Feed fresh and limited. Drying does not remove oxalate.
