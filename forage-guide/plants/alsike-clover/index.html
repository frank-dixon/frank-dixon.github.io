Legume Summer

Trifolium hybridum

A clover with a poor reputation because of liver and photosensitivity disease, best documented in horses.

Uncertain: Disease reports are strongest for horses, which this guide does not cover. The limit for the other species is a caution, not a count of cases.

Who can eat it

  • Rabbit Limit Uncertain

    Alsike clover is linked to photosensitivity and liver disease, with the clearest reports in horses. Other stock should not get it as a chosen forage.

    A trace in mixed hay is a different situation from a bundle of alsike. Do not harvest it on purpose.

Identification

Upright, hairless, with a flower head that is white and pink on the same plant. Leaflets lack the white crescent of white clover.

Lookalikes

White clover creeps and the flower stalk is leafless. Red clover is hairy.

Parts to feed

Not a chosen feed. If it is mixed into hay, it should not dominate the bale.

New England timing

Flowers in midsummer. It shows up in New England hay mixes and on damp ground.

Harvest

Do not cut a pure stand to feed.

Preparation

Drying does not make a heavy alsike cutting a good feed.
