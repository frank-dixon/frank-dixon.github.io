Legume Summer

Melilotus officinalis · white sweet clover is the same caution

A tall roadside legume. Moldy sweet-clover hay causes a clotting failure.

Who can eat it

  • Rabbit Limit

    Moldy sweet clover makes dicoumarol, which stops blood from clotting. Cattle are the classic victims. Fresh, unspoiled plants do not have that toxin, but they are not a chosen staple.

    If the cutting smells moldy or heated, do not feed it to any animal.

Identification

Tall, branched, with small yellow flowers in narrow clusters and a sweet smell when crushed. Leaves are in threes.

Lookalikes

White sweet clover is the same problem with white flowers. Alfalfa has purple flowers and does not smell the same.

Parts to feed

Not a preferred fresh feed. Spoiled hay is dangerous.

New England timing

Tall plants flower along New England roadsides in July and August. The bleeding problem is in spoiled hay, not in a fresh handful.

Harvest

Do not bale it wet. Do not feed stems that heated or molded.

Preparation

Fresh plants are a limited green. Any moldy sweet-clover hay should be thrown out, not diluted.
