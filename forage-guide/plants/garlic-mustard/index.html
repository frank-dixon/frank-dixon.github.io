Forb Spring

Alliaria petiolata

An invasive mustard. It is not a studied livestock feed, and it smells of garlic.

Uncertain: Livestock feeding trials are thin. The limit is a caution based on its chemistry, not a list of poisoning cases.

Who can eat it

  • Rabbit Limit Uncertain

    Garlic mustard contains mustard oils and garlic-like compounds. It is not a documented staple feed, and Allium relatives cause blood damage in several species.

    Leave it out of the mix. Removing it from a woods is still worth doing; just do not feed the bag.

Identification

Heart-shaped toothed leaves, a garlic smell when crushed, and small white flowers with four petals in the second year.

Lookalikes

Violets do not smell of garlic. Ground ivy creeps and has scalloped round leaves.

Parts to feed

Not a recommended feed. Pulling it for disposal is a different job from feeding it.

New England timing

Rosettes overwinter. The best leaves are April and May, before the white flowers set seed. It is a serious New England woodland invasive.

Harvest

Do not add it to a forage basket.

Preparation

There is no preparation step that turns it into a recommended feed.
