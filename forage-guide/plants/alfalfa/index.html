Legume Spring Summer

Medicago sativa

A planted hay legume, not a common wild plant, but ordinary on New England farms and in hay.

Who can eat it

  • Rabbit Limit

    House Rabbit Society treats alfalfa hay as suitable for growing rabbits and a limited hay for adults because of the calcium.

    Fresh alfalfa is gassier than hay. Introduce it slowly.

  • Chicken Limit

    Alfalfa is rich feed. Lush stands cause frothy bloat in cattle, sheep, and goats. Adult rabbits get more calcium than they need from a diet of alfalfa hay.

    Dried hay is appropriate for growing rabbits. For adult rabbits, timothy or orchardgrass should be the usual hay. Ruminants should not get a sudden meal of wet alfalfa.

  • Cattle Limit

    Alfalfa is a standard dairy forage and a classic cause of frothy bloat when it is lush.

    Wilt a fresh cutting. Do not turn hungry cattle onto a pure wet stand.

Identification

Three leaflets, the center one on a short stalk, and purple flower clusters. The seed pods spiral.

Lookalikes

Black medic is a low relative with yellow flowers and black pods. Clovers lack the stalked center leaflet.

Parts to feed

Leaves and fine stems. Mature stems are woody.

New England timing

First cut is often late May or June in southern New England, later north. Later cuts follow through the summer if the stand is irrigated or the season is wet.

Harvest

Cut at bud or early flower for the best feed. Lush wet alfalfa is a bloat risk for ruminants.

Preparation

Dried alfalfa hay is the usual form for rabbits. Wilt fresh cuttings before ruminants get them. Do not feed moldy hay.
