Rubus allegheniensis · bramble, black raspberry leaves are used the same way

A bramble. Leaves are a standard rabbit green and goat browse. Other Rubus species in the region, including black raspberry, are used the same way.

Who can eat it

  • Rabbit Safe

    Bramble leaves are a common rabbit green and a normal goat browse. They are not a toxicology problem.

    Watch for poison ivy growing through the patch. Do not grab canes bare-handed.

Identification

Arching canes with thorns, leaves usually in fives, and white flowers followed by black aggregate fruit that does not slip off a core.

Lookalikes

Multiflora rose has a different flower and a hip, not a blackberry. Poison ivy can thread through a bramble patch and has three glossy leaflets and no thorns.

Parts to feed

Leaves and young cane tips. Fruit is fine for poultry and pigs in season. Thorns are the practical limit for the person cutting it and for wool.

New England timing

Leaves are useful from May until frost. Fruit ripens in late July and August in much of New England, later in the north.

Harvest

Clip leafy tips with gloves. Leave the old woody cane.

Preparation

Feed fresh or dry the leaves. No wilting is required for safety.
