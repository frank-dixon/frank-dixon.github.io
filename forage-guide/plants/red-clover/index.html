Legume Spring Summer

Trifolium pratense

A hay and pasture clover, upright, with pink-purple heads.

Who can eat it

  • Rabbit Limit

    Red clover shares the bloat risk of other clovers. It also contains phytoestrogens. A leaf fungus (black patch) is tied to slobbers, which is saliva and irritation best known in horses.

    Use a small share of a mix. Do not feed blotched, moldy clover.

Identification

Three leaflets with a pale mark, hairy stems, and a pink head sitting close to the upper leaves.

Lookalikes

Alsike clover is finer, not hairy, with a pink-and-white head. White clover creeps.

Parts to feed

Leaves and young flower heads, sparingly.

New England timing

Cut leaf and early flower from June into August. Plants with brown patches on the leaves may carry the fungus associated with slobbers.

Harvest

Skip plants with lots of brown leaf blotches. Do not make it the whole cutting.

Preparation

Wilt before feeding ruminants. Dry hay is safer than lush wet clover. Discard mold.
