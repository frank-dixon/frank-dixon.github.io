Salix spp.

A standard browse for rabbits and goats. Salicylates are the reason not to feed a diet of bark.

Who can eat it

  • Rabbit Safe

    Willow is widely used rabbit and goat browse. The salicylates in the bark are a reason to limit bark, not a reason to avoid a normal twig.

    A few twigs are the usual offer. Do not feed a diet of willow bark.

Identification

Alternate, narrow leaves on flexible twigs, often along water. Buds are covered by a single scale.

Lookalikes

Brittle willow lookalikes are still willows. Cherry twigs have a bitter-almond smell when scratched and are a different, toxic entry.

Parts to feed

Twigs and leaves, in moderation. Not a pile of bark.

New England timing

Twigs are cut from late winter through summer. Pussy willow (Salix discolor) and black willow (Salix nigra) are common New England species. Leaves are best before midsummer.

Harvest

Cut finger-thick twigs. Do not girdle a tree you want to keep.

Preparation

Feed fresh. Dried leafy twigs are fine. Do not feed a large amount of bark.
