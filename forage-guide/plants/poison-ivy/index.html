Verdict for Rabbit, Chicken, Duck, Goose, Turkey, Quail, Guinea pig, Goat, Sheep, Pig, Cattle

Colorado State University and the University of Arkansas Extension say animals are unaffected by poison ivy. We keep it cautious because the oil on coats and in manure harms the people who handle them, and we found no source that confirms it is safe feed for every animal listed here.

  • Colorado State University College of Veterinary Medicine, Guide to Poisonous Plants: Poison ivy (Toxicodendron radicans (L.) Kuntze)
    Animals affected: Animals are not affected by poison ivy. People are primarily sensitive to the irritant oil in the plant. Toxic principle: The leaves and stems contain an irritant oil called urushiol that is released from the crushed or broken surface of the leaves or stems. Special notes: Poison ivy is usually not a…
  • University of Arkansas Cooperative Extension Service: Plants Poisonous to Livestock in the Southern US
    Poison Ivy (Toxicodendron radicans) • Toxic Principle: phenolic compound urushiol • Mucous and membrane irritant • No effect on livestock. • Breathing smoke may cause irritation.

Do not feed it to them. Details are under Who can eat it.

Toxicodendron radicans

Not a cut-and-carry plant. The person cutting it is the one who gets hurt. Goats often eat it and are a special case below.

Who can eat it

  • Rabbit Toxic

    Kept cautious. Colorado State and Arkansas Extension say livestock are not affected, but we have not found a source that clears poison ivy as feed for each animal here. The oil, urushiol, stays on coats and in manure and blisters people who handle them.

    Do not touch it. Do not put it in the feed.

Identification

Three leaflets, the center one on a longer stalk, often shiny, and a woody vine or low shrub. White berries late in the year. No thorns.

Lookalikes

Virginia creeper usually has five leaflets. Blackberry has thorns and five leaflets. Raspberry has thorns. When in doubt, do not touch it.

Parts to feed

Do not cut it by hand for any animal.

New England timing

It leafs out in May and is a handling hazard until the leaves are gone. Vines and low plants are both common across New England.

Harvest

Do not put it in a basket, a wheelbarrow of greens, or a hay pile.

Preparation

There is no safe handling method offered here.
