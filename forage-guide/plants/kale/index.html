Garden Spring Summer Fall Winter

Brassica oleracea · cabbage leaves, collards, broccoli leaves

A cold-hardy garden brassica. Gas and goitrogens are the limit, and kale anemia is a cattle problem when the intake is high.

Who can eat it

  • Rabbit Safe

    House Rabbit Society lists kale (all types) among the leafy greens that are safe for rabbits. Introduce it slowly, like any new green.

    A leaf or two for a rabbit, or a small share of a mix, is the usual limit. Do not switch cattle onto a field of kale all at once.

  • Chicken Limit

    Brassicas cause gas in rabbits and can cause anemia and thyroid trouble in ruminants when they become a large share of the diet.

    A leaf or two for a rabbit, or a small share of a mix, is the usual limit. Do not switch cattle onto a field of kale all at once.

  • Duck Limit Uncertain

Identification

Blue-green curly or flat leaves on a plant you planted. Broccoli and cabbage leaves count as this entry.

Lookalikes

Comfrey is hairy with bell flowers and is toxic. Do not grab a large garden leaf without knowing it.

Parts to feed

Leaves, in moderation.

New England timing

Spring plantings are cut from June. Fall plantings hold into November and, in southern New England, through many winters. Cabbage, collards, and broccoli leaves are the same species and the same limit.

Harvest

Cut leaves, not a wheelbarrow of them for a few rabbits.

Preparation

Feed fresh and limited. Do not feed a sudden large amount to cattle or sheep.
