Toxic parts

Toxic parts: Cornell lists leaves, twigs, roots and unripe fruit. Colorado State says all parts are poisonous, especially when wilted. We show the Cornell list.

  • Cornell University Department of Animal Science, Plants Poisonous to Livestock: Sambucus canadensis (Elderberry)
    Sambucus canadensis. Species most often affected: cattle, humans, goats. Poisonous parts: leaves, twigs, roots, unripe fruits. Primary poisons: sambunigrin.
  • NC State Extension, North Carolina Extension Gardener Plant Toolbox: Sambucus canadensis (American Elder, American Elderberry, Common Elderberry, Elderberry)
    Poisonous to humans. Poison severity: Low. Poison part: Fruits, Leaves, Roots, Stems. Toxic principle: Cyanogenic glycoside and alkaloid.
  • Colorado State University College of Veterinary Medicine, Guide to Poisonous Plants: Elderberry (Sambucus L.)
    Animals affected: Cattle, sheep Toxic principle: Cyanogenic glycoside sambunigrin which is hydrolysed in the rumen by microorganisms to free hydrogen cyanide (HCN). Sambucus canadensis

A common shrub. The leafy growth is not forage. Ripe fruit is a human food only after it is cooked, which is outside this guide.

Who can eat it

  • Rabbit Toxic

    Leaves, stems, bark, and unripe berries contain cyanogenic glycosides. They are not livestock forage.

    Do not feed vegetative growth. This guide does not recommend the fruit as animal feed.

Identification

A shrub with opposite compound leaves, white flat flower clusters, and dark berries. Twigs have a white pith.

Lookalikes

Pokeweed is an herb with alternate leaves and a red stem, and it is also toxic. Water hemlock is an herb, not a shrub, and is deadly.

Parts to feed

Do not feed leaves, bark, or unripe berries.

New England timing

It flowers white in June and July in New England wet edges. Purple-black fruit follows. Leaves, stems, and unripe fruit are the cyanide problem.

Harvest

Do not strip leafy stems for a basket.

Preparation

Do not dry the leaves for feed.
