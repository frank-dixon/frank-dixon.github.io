Grass Spring Summer

Phalaris arundinacea

A tall wetland grass along New England ditches, streams, and wet meadows.

Uncertain: Alkaloid level depends on the variety, which a wild stand does not reveal.

Who can eat it

    Some reed canarygrass contains gramine and related alkaloids that reduce intake and have caused toxicity. Variety cannot be told in the field.

    Do not use a pure stand as the diet.

Identification

Broad leaves and a straw-colored, open seed head. It forms dense stands in wet soil. Ligule is prominent.

Lookalikes

Orchardgrass is an upland clump grass. Reed canarygrass is wetter and taller.

Parts to feed

Young leaves only, and only in a mix.

New England timing

It is up early in wet ground, from May, and is coarse by July. Older varieties contain alkaloids. Low-alkaloid varieties exist and look the same.

Harvest

Cut before it becomes a coarse cane. Leave the wet roots alone.

Preparation

Hay from high-alkaloid stands is still a poor feed. Do not assume drying makes it safe.
