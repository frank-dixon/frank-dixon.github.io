Forb Summer

Chenopodium album

A common garden weed. Poultry and pigs eat it. Nitrate and oxalate set the limit.

Who can eat it

  • Rabbit Limit

    Lambsquarters accumulates nitrate on rich soil and also contains oxalate. It has been a useful poultry green in modest amounts and a cattle problem in heavy, stressed stands.

    Do not feed a large cutting from fertilized garden beds.

  • Chicken Limit

Identification

Diamond-shaped leaves with a white mealy coating, especially on the growing tips.

Lookalikes

Pigweed (Amaranthus) has a coarser stem and no white meal on the tip. Nightshades have different flowers and berries and are toxic.

Parts to feed

Young tops only, and not from heavily fertilized ground.

New England timing

From June until it goes to seed in late summer. Plants on rich garden soil can accumulate nitrate.

Harvest

Cut young plants. Skip thick stands on old manure piles.

Preparation

Feed a limited fresh amount. Do not ferment a large mass of it.
