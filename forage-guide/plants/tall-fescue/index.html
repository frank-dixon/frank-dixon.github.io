Lolium arundinaceum · Schedonorus arundinaceus

A tough pasture grass. The endophyte, not the leaf shape, is the feeding problem.

Uncertain: Endophyte status of a given plant cannot be judged by eye.

Who can eat it

  • Rabbit Limit Uncertain

    Tall fescue endophyte produces ergovaline. In cattle and sheep it is tied to heat stress, poor gain, and reproductive trouble. You cannot see the infection.

    A little in a mixed pasture is common. Do not feed a heavy stand as the only forage. Endophyte-free and novel-endophyte varieties exist, but a plant in a field does not show which it is.

Identification

Coarse dark leaves, prominent veins, and often a reddish base. It grows in clumps.

Lookalikes

Orchardgrass has a flattened stem and a branched seed head. Reed canarygrass prefers wetter soil and has a more open seed head.

Parts to feed

Leaves, if you use it at all. Do not make a suspect stand the whole diet.

New England timing

It grows from May through October and stays green late. Many older New England pastures are endophyte-infected. The fungus is invisible.

Harvest

If you cut it, keep the amount small and mix it with other forage.

Preparation

Drying does not remove the endophyte toxin.
