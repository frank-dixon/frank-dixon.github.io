A lawn and path weed fed as a minor green.

Identification

Broad oval leaves with parallel veins, in a rosette. The flower is a green spike. English plantain has lance leaves and a similar spike.

Lookalikes

Some lilies send up strap leaves, but they lack the parallel-veined rosette and the green spike. If the plant is not clearly plantain, leave it.

Parts to feed

Leaves of both common and English plantain.

New England timing

Leaves are usable from May until frost. They get stringy in late summer. English plantain (Plantago lanceolata) is the narrow-leaved relative and is used the same way.

Harvest

Cut leaves, not the tough flower spikes. Avoid sprayed lawns.

Preparation

Feed fresh or dried. No special wilting.
