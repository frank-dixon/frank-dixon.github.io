Tree Toxic to some animals Spring Fall

Quercus alba

Oaks are common in southern and central New England and in warmer pockets north. Tannins are the problem.

Who can eat it

  • Rabbit Limit

    Oak buds, young leaves, and acorns are high in tannins. They cause gut and kidney disease when they become a large part of the diet.

    A mouthful of mature leaf is not the same as a diet of acorns. Do not feed acorns as a crop.

  • Chicken Limit

    Oak buds, young leaves, and acorns are high in tannins. They cause gut and kidney disease when they become a large part of the diet.

    A mouthful of mature leaf is not the same as a diet of acorns. Do not feed acorns as a crop.

  • Duck Limit Uncertain

    Oak buds, young leaves, and acorns are high in tannins. They cause gut and kidney disease when they become a large part of the diet.

    A mouthful of mature leaf is not the same as a diet of acorns. Do not feed acorns as a crop. This follows poultry guidance used for chickens. A duck-specific reference was not used, so it is marked uncertain.

  • Guinea pig Limit Uncertain

    Oak buds, young leaves, and acorns are high in tannins. They cause gut and kidney disease when they become a large part of the diet.

    A mouthful of mature leaf is not the same as a diet of acorns. Do not feed acorns as a crop. Guinea-pig trials for this plant are limited, so this follows the rabbit verdict and is marked uncertain.

  • Goat Limit

    Oak buds, young leaves, and acorns are high in tannins. They cause gut and kidney disease when they become a large part of the diet.

    A mouthful of mature leaf is not the same as a diet of acorns. Do not feed acorns as a crop.

  • Sheep Toxic

    Sheep are poisoned by the same oak tannins that affect cattle, especially on a heavy acorn drop.

    Do not feed acorns or young leaves.

  • Pig Limit

    Oak buds, young leaves, and acorns are high in tannins. They cause gut and kidney disease when they become a large part of the diet.

    A mouthful of mature leaf is not the same as a diet of acorns. Do not feed acorns as a crop.

  • Cattle Toxic

    Cattle are regularly poisoned by acorns and young oak leaves. Kidney damage and constipation or diarrhea are the picture.

    Keep cattle off a heavy acorn crop and do not feed oak cuttings.

Identification

Alternate lobed leaves. White oak has rounded lobes. Red oak has pointed lobes. Acorns sit in a cap.

Lookalikes

Maple leaves are opposite. Beech has smooth bark and a simple toothless leaf edge that is not lobed like an oak.

Parts to feed

Not young leaves, and not a meal of acorns.

New England timing

Young leaves in May and June and acorns in September and October are the risky parts. Red oaks (pointed lobes) are often worse than white oaks. Both are limited here.

Harvest

Do not shake a tree full of green acorns into a pen.

Preparation

There is no home step that removes tannins from a pile of acorns.
