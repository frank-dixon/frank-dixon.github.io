Grid List
  • Artist's conk Ganoderma applanatum Hard, flat shelves on dead and dying hardwoods; lasts for years.
  • Birch polypore Fomitopsis betulina Only on dead or dying birch.
  • Bitter bolete Tylopilus felleus On soil or well-rotted wood under hemlock and hardwoods.
  • Black trumpet Craterellus fallax In moss and leaf litter under oak and beech, often on slopes.
  • Bleeding tooth Hydnellum peckii On soil under conifers.
  • Chaga Inonotus obliquus On living birch trunks in northern forests.
  • Chanterelle Cantharellus species Singly or scattered on soil under oak, beech, and other hardwoods in the East; under Douglas-fir and other conifers in the West.
  • Chicken of the woods Laetiporus sulphureus Overlapping shelves on living or dead hardwoods, especially oak.
  • Common ink cap Coprinopsis atramentaria Poisonous Clusters in grass, often over buried wood or roots.
  • Deadly galerina Galerina marginata Deadly On rotting wood, especially conifer logs, often among moss.
  • Death cap Amanita phalloides Deadly On soil under oaks and introduced trees; common in coastal California and spreading in the Pacific Northwest. Rare and local in the Northeast.
  • Destroying angel Amanita bisporigera (East), A. ocreata (California) Deadly On soil in woods and at wood edges, sometimes in lawns near trees.
  • Dryad's saddle (pheasant back) Cerioporus squamosus On dead or dying elm, maple, and other hardwoods.
  • Fairy ring mushroom Marasmius oreades In rings or arcs in lawns and pastures, often with a ring of darker grass.
  • False morel Gyromitra esculenta Poisonous On soil under conifers, especially pine and aspen-pine woods, in spring.
  • Fly agaric Amanita muscaria Poisonous On soil under conifers and birch, often in rings.
  • Gem-studded puffball Lycoperdon perlatum On soil and leaf litter in woods, along trails, and sometimes in grass.
  • Giant puffball Calvatia gigantea Lawns, pastures, field edges, and open woods.
  • Green-spored parasol Chlorophyllum molybdites Poisonous Lawns and parks, often in large rings or arcs.
  • Hemlock varnish shelf Ganoderma tsugae On dead or dying eastern hemlock, sometimes other conifers.
  • Hen of the woods (maitake) Grifola frondosa A large rosette at the base of oaks, usually living ones, often in the same spot each year.
  • Honey mushroom Armillaria mellea group Dense clusters at the base of living or dead trees and stumps, or from buried roots.
  • Jack-o'-lantern Omphalotus illudens Poisonous Clusters at the base of hardwood stumps, especially oak, or from buried roots.
  • King bolete Boletus edulis group On soil under spruce, pine, fir, and hemlock; in places also birch and oak.
  • Lion's mane Hericium erinaceus On wounds of living hardwoods or dead logs, especially oak, beech, and maple.
  • Lobster mushroom Hypomyces lactifluorum On soil in mixed woods; it is a mold that covers certain Russula and Lactifluus mushrooms.
  • Meadow mushroom Agaricus campestris Lawns, pastures, and grassy fields, often after rain.
  • Morel Morchella species On soil near dying elms, ash, old apple orchards, and tulip poplar; in the West, also on burns.
  • Old man of the woods Strobilomyces strobilaceus group On soil under oaks and other hardwoods.
  • Oyster mushroom Pleurotus ostreatus group Overlapping shelves on dead or dying hardwoods.
  • Pigskin poison puffball (earthball) Scleroderma citrinum Poisonous On soil or rotting wood under hardwoods and conifers.
  • Shaggy mane Coprinus comatus Lawns, roadsides, wood chips, and disturbed ground, often in groups.
  • Stinkhorns Phallus ravenelii and Mutinus elegans In wood chips, mulch, and rich soil, often in gardens.
  • Sulfur tuft Hypholoma fasciculare Poisonous Dense clusters on stumps and logs, hardwood and conifer.
  • Turkey tail Trametes versicolor Overlapping shelves on dead hardwood logs, stumps, and branches.
  • Velvet foot Flammulina velutipes In clusters on dead hardwood, especially elm and willow, in cold weather and even winter thaws.
  • Wine cap Stropharia rugosoannulata In wood chips, garden beds, and mulched paths.
  • Witch's butter Tremella mesenterica On dead hardwood branches, often appearing after rain in cool weather.
  • Wood ear Auricularia americana group On dead hardwood or conifer branches and logs.
