Ameraucana× Black Copper Marans
Expected eggOlive green

Purebred Ameraucanas carry two copies of the dominant blue allele, so every first-generation daughter gets one and lays a blue shell under heavy brown pigment: olive. Brown-only layers can appear in later generations.

Breeder-reported Koops Coops

Cream Legbar× French Cuckoo Marans
Expected eggSage to olive green

Koops Coops crossed Cream Legbar with French Cuckoo Marans for olive eggers. The shade varies between sage and olive.

The source used French Cuckoo Marans, not Black Copper Marans.

Breeder-reported Koops Coops

Cream Legbar× Welsummer
Expected eggGreen, possibly speckled

Koops Coops crossed these to aim for speckled green eggs. Speckling is not guaranteed.

Breeder-reported Koops Coops

Ameraucana× Rhode Island Red
Expected eggBlue/green mix (Easter Egger type)

Crossing a brown layer with a blue layer produces Easter Eggers. Egg color varies from hen to hen.

Breeder-reported Koops Coops

Araucana× White Leghorn
Expected eggBlue

Leghorns add no brown pigment, so daughters carrying the dominant blue allele should lay blue (inferred from genetics, not reported by a breeder).

About Shellwork

A reference to chicken breeds by the eggs they lay. Figures come from The Livestock Conservancy, McMurray Hatchery, Koops Coops and peer-reviewed genetics papers, and each breed page lists its sources. Photos are openly licensed from Wikimedia Commons and credited where they appear. Egg colors on screen are approximate.
