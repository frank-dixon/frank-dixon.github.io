Glossary

Autosexing
A breed whose chicks can be sexed at hatch by down color or pattern, e.g. Cream Legbar. Source: Koops Coops
Biliverdin
The blue-green pigment in blue eggshells. Source: PLOS Genetics
Broody
A broody hen wants to sit on eggs and hatch them. Breeds differ: some rarely go broody, others often do. Hatcheries rate how likely each breed is. Source: Koops Coops
Cuticle / bloom
The thin organic coat on a freshly laid egg. On brown eggs it carries much of the pigment. Source: Poultry Science
EAV-HP
An endogenous avian retrovirus. Its insertion near SLCO1B3 is the cause of blue eggs, found in both Chinese and Mapuche fowl. Source: PLOS ONE
Easter Egger
A cross of a regular brown layer and a blue layer, not a recognized breed. Egg color varies from hen to hen. Source: Koops Coops
Heritage breed
A traditional breed tracked on The Livestock Conservancy priority list (Critical, Watch, Recovering, etc.). Source: The Livestock Conservancy
Olive egg
A blue shell with brown pigment over it. Usually from a dark-brown × blue cross. Source: Koops Coops
Oocyan (O) allele
The dominant blue-eggshell allele. One copy makes the whole shell blue, inside and out. Source: PLOS Genetics
Protoporphyrin IX
The brown pigment, laid mainly in the outer shell and cuticle. It usually lightens as a hen ages. Source: Poultry Science
SLCO1B3
A transporter gene. The blue-shell insertion switches it on in the shell gland so that biliverdin is taken up into the shell. Source: PLOS Genetics

About Shellwork

A reference to chicken breeds by the eggs they lay. Figures come from The Livestock Conservancy, McMurray Hatchery, Koops Coops and peer-reviewed genetics papers, and each breed page lists its sources. Photos are openly licensed from Wikimedia Commons and credited where they appear. Egg colors on screen are approximate.
