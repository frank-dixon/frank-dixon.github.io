Black Australorp

Egg colorLight brown to brown
Egg sizeLarge · Large (McMurray, Koops); medium to large (Conservancy)
Egg shapeVaries by hen · not sourced
Eggs per year226–300
TemperamentActive, yet gentle
BroodinessSources differ: rarely goes broody (McMurray) or often broody (Koops)
AutosexingUnknown
OriginAustralia (from English Black Orpingtons)

How the shell gets its color

Brown shell from protoporphyrin IX (see glossary).

Sources

  1. Koops Coops: Black Australorp Accessed Oct 15, 2026. Supports: eggs per year
    Brown; Large; 250-300 per year
  2. McMurray Hatchery: Black Australorps Accessed Oct 15, 2026. Supports: origin, egg color, eggs per year, broodiness
    Developed from Black Orpingtons imported from England; brown; large; average 226 per year
  3. Poultry Science: Samiullah S., Roberts J.R., Chousalkar K. (2015) Eggshell color in brown-egg laying hens — a review Accessed Oct 15, 2026. Supports: genetics
    Brown color is protoporphyrin IX, concentrated in the outer shell and cuticle; color intensity tends to decline with hen age.
  4. The Livestock Conservancy: Australorp Chicken Accessed Oct 15, 2026. Supports: egg color, egg size, temperament
    Tinted to Light Brown; Medium to Large; Active, yet gentle

Egg shape: no source gives a typical shape for this breed, so it’s listed as “varies by hen.” Colors on screen are approximate.
