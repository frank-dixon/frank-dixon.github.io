Your browser doesn’t animate on scroll timelines yet — the essay still reads fine; the scrubbed bar is a progressive enhancement.

CSS craft · no scroll listeners

Scroll-scrubbed reading progress

A quiet editorial demo: the top bar and chapter dots track how far you’ve read using animation-timeline: scroll() and named view timelines — not IntersectionObserver, not a scroll handler.

1. Intent

Most “reading progress” widgets listen to scroll, compute a ratio, and write a width on every frame. It works. It’s also a tiny tax on every publisher site, and it couples layout to JavaScript for something the browser can express declaratively now.

This page keeps the essay first. The chrome is progressive enhancement: if scroll-driven animations aren’t available, you still get a readable article and calm chapter links.

Goal: show FE taste — replace a JS habit with platform CSS, without punishing older browsers.

2. Engine

The bar uses animation-timeline: scroll(root) with a simple scaleX keyframe from 0 to 1. The timeline is the document’s scroll progress, so the fill is scrubbed by the scroller itself — no rAF loop.

Chapter dots bind to named view-timelines on each section. As a chapter enters and settles in view, its dot fills and the label brightens. Same family of APIs, different subject: scrollport vs element.

Hash links still jump to chapters the boring, reliable way. That’s fine. The motion is optional seasoning, not the navigation contract.

3. Fallback

@supports (animation-timeline: scroll()) gates the scrubbing. Outside that fence, the bar sits as a quiet hint and the note under the dots explains why nothing is animating.

prefers-reduced-motion: reduce kills the scrub animations and leaves a subdued full-width track so motion-sensitive readers aren’t chased by a growing bar.

That’s the craft signal: enhancement stacked on a solid document, not a demo that only works in the newest Chromium build.

4. Taste

Shared hub header, same tokens as the rest of the site, evening-sized scope. No framework, no build, no observer soup. Just enough essay length to feel the scrub — then stop.

If you’re hiring for judgment: this is the kind of small page that shows whether someone reaches for the platform before they reach for a listener.

Ship rule for this demo: one idea, one URL, zero upkeep.
